Prompt Teacup is a project to build a spiritual successor to Escape Velocity, the 1996 Macintosh game from Ambrosia Software. All of the IP for that game is public domain, but this is not a remaster, sequel, prequel, or even a game set in the same universe. This is a separate work that shares a great deal with it's ancestor.

Specifically, the core gameplay patterns will be essentially identical. In both games, the player's screen is from an "Eye in the sky" perspective, looking down on the ship which they are piloting. As far as the player is concerned, his ship, and all entities in the world, move in only two dimensions. Information is conveyed to the player by means of panels displaying things such as ship health, cargo, current star system, hyperspace navigation target, etc. The player will be primarily engaged in some combination of combat with other space ships, transporting and trading cargo, and completing missions, both procedural and designed. There is a central story to pursue, as well numerous smaller stories, which vary in duration and complexity. Some are one off individual missions, others are sequences of several missions. Some missions block or unlock other missions. The game world exists on two levels: within a star system, and the network of star systems. The player flies his ship around within star systems, and may "jump" from one star system to another. Star systems contain between zero and a few stellar objects, which may be landed on. Generally, all of the activities other than combat, such as trading cargo and advancing missions, happens when landed. Combat happens exclusively while flying. Star systems have between zero and a few hyperspace links connecting the star system to nearby star systems. All links are the same distance - one jump. The primary way to travel between star systems is to jump from one star system to the next via these links. Taken together, this network of star systems forms the galactic map. Combat is "Arcade-style", and not intended to be representative of "real" space ship combat, whatever that might be.

Teacup differs significantly from it's ancestor in presentation. The game is rendered in 3d, for a variety of reasons. The visual aesthetic is a collage of retro-futurist styles inspired by various works of fiction such as Star Wars and Tron, and historical technologies such vacuum fluorescent displays. The "Rule of cool" supercedes any requirement for a technologicaly viable explanation, although where technological explanations enhance the experience, they are highly encouraged.

There will also be several additions, gameplay-wise, to Teacup, not found in it's ancestor. These are not exact requirements, and may not all find their way into the production game. One example is crew members. In Teacup, the player will be able to take on crewmembers that add dialogue and enhance the performance of ship systems, such as weapons. These crewmembers may be humans, independent robots like R2D2, software packages such as AI that integrates with the ship like Mother or Hal, or non-human races. Another example is locational/module damage. In Escape Velocity, the ship could take only two types of damage - shields, and armor. There is very little effective difference between the two, and where the ship is hit is irrelevent. In Teacup, the physical part of the ship that is hit by an incoming weapon will determine what part of the ship is damaged. This has numerous gameplay implications.

It hardly bares mentioning, but there will also be some things not carried forward. There will never be a concrete list of what was not carried forward - suffice to say that there will be no effort to preserve every element of Escape Velocity.
