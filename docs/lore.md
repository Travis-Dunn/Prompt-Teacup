1970's idea of what 100 years in the future might look like.

Two main factions: one descended from the USSR, one from NATO. They share one critical commonality - both of them manage to reign in their worst tendancies enough that they don't collapse. They aren't gone - and they play a primary role in shaping their evolution over the years, but they manage to stabilize and keep things going. 

The USSR becomes even more internationalist and less Russian dominated, eventually merging with China, Vietnam, Angola, and many, many more.

The other bloc, "The Free World", also evolves more toward a loose alliance of democratic peers, rather than being dominated by the USA. They continue to be united by maintaining voluntary membership to a multi-national governing body.

Highly miniaturized microprocessors are never developed, but computer science does continue. Nuclear science, and most other technologies, continue apace. AI does exist - but it looks more like Mother from Alien than HAL from 2001.

Worldbuilding is distinctly soft, and while nitty gritty technological details are welcome where they serve a purpose, they are not in themselves the focus. The rule of cool dictates what's possible, not our current understanding of physics. FTL is about as hard as supersonic flight was in 1970. Which is to say, ubiquitous within developed nations, but still not cheap or without tradeoffs.
